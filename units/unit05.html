UNIT 05 · MID TERM

Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis

1. What are Moments?

Definition: In statistics, a moment is a quantitative measure that describes important characteristics of a data distribution.

Moments describe:

  • Central tendency (mean)
  • Dispersion (variance)
  • Skewness (asymmetry)
  • Kurtosis (peakedness or flatness)

That is, moments are a set of statistical parameters used to measure a distribution. The shape of any distribution can be described by its various "moments".

2. Raw Moments and Central Moments

Raw moment: A moment about any arbitrary value \(A\) is known as a raw moment.
\(r\)th Raw Moment (about \(A\))
\[\mu_r' = \frac{\sum (x - A)^r}{n}\]
Central moment: A moment about the mean is known as a central moment.
\(r\)th Central Moment (about \(\bar{x}\))
\[\mu_r = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^r}{n}\]
Special Case

When \(A = 0\), the raw moment is taken about the origin: \(\mu_r' = \dfrac{\sum x^r}{n}\). The first raw moment about the origin is simply the mean.

3. The First Four Central Moments

First Central Moment
\[\mu_1 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^1}{n}\]
Second Central Moment
\[\mu_2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n}\]
Third Central Moment
\[\mu_3 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^3}{n}\]
Fourth Central Moment
\[\mu_4 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^4}{n}\]

Why \(\mu_1 = 0\)

The first central moment is always zero
\[\mu_1 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^1}{n} = \frac{\sum x - n\bar{x}}{n} = \frac{n\bar{x} - n\bar{x}}{n} = 0\]

Because \(\sum x = n\bar{x}\), the positive and negative deviations from the mean cancel exactly. This is precisely why the mean absolute deviation uses \(|x - \bar{x}|\) and the variance uses \((x-\bar{x})^2\).

Why \(\mu_2 = \text{Variance}\)

The second central moment is the variance
\[\mu_2 = \frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{n} = \sigma^2\]
Summary

\(\mu_1 = 0\)  ·  \(\mu_2 = \sigma^2\)  ·  \(\mu_3\) drives skewness  ·  \(\mu_4\) drives kurtosis

4. Skewness

Definition: Skewness measures the asymmetry of a distribution — whether the data is symmetrically distributed or leans more towards one side of the mean.
Type Tail Relationship
Positive skewnessLong tail to the rightMean > Median > Mode
Zero skewnessSymmetricMean = Median = Mode
Negative skewnessLong tail to the leftMean < Median < Mode

5. Measure of Skewness Using Moments

The measure of skewness may also be obtained by making use of moments. A relative measure of skewness is denoted by \(\beta_1\) and is defined as:

Moment Coefficient of Skewness
\[\beta_1 = \frac{\mu_3}{\sigma^3}\]

Since \(\sigma = \sqrt{\mu_2}\), this can equivalently be written \(\beta_1 = \dfrac{\mu_3}{\mu_2^{3/2}}\).

Interpretation

  • If \(\beta_1 = 0\) → the distribution is symmetrical
  • If \(\beta_1 > 0\) (+ve) → the distribution is positively skewed
  • If \(\beta_1 < 0\) (−ve) → the distribution is negatively skewed
↗️

Positive Skewness

Right-tailed. A few unusually large values pull the mean up.

⚖️

Zero Skewness

Symmetric distribution — the normal curve is the classic case.

↘️

Negative Skewness

Left-tailed. A few unusually small values pull the mean down.

Alternative (Pearson) Measures
\[Sk = \frac{\text{Mean} - \text{Mode}}{\sigma}\] \[Sk = \frac{3(\text{Mean} - \text{Median})}{\sigma}\]

6. Kurtosis

Definition: Kurtosis refers to the pointedness (peakedness) of a peak in the distribution curve. The main difference between skewness and kurtosis is that the former talks of the degree of symmetry, whereas the latter talks of the degree of peakedness in the frequency distribution.

There are three types of kurtosis: mesokurtic, leptokurtic and platykurtic.

🔺

Leptokurtic

A curve having a relatively higher peak than the normal curve. Kurtosis > 3 — heavy tails, more outliers.

🔔

Mesokurtic

The normal curve itself is called mesokurtic. Kurtosis = 3.

🛖

Platykurtic

A curve having a relatively flat peak than the normal curve. Kurtosis < 3 — light tails, few outliers.

7. Measure of Kurtosis Using Moments

The measure of kurtosis may also be obtained by making use of moments. A relative measure of kurtosis is denoted by \(\beta_2\) and is defined as:

Moment Coefficient of Kurtosis
\[\beta_2 = \frac{\mu_4}{\mu_2^2}\]

Since \(\mu_2 = \sigma^2\), this is equivalently \(\beta_2 = \dfrac{\mu_4}{\sigma^4}\).

Interpretation

  • If \(\beta_2 = 3\) → the distribution is mesokurtic
  • If \(\beta_2 > 3\) → the distribution is leptokurtic
  • If \(\beta_2 < 3\) → the distribution is platykurtic
Excess Kurtosis

Some texts report excess kurtosis, \(\beta_2 - 3\), so that the normal distribution scores 0 instead of 3. Check which convention a question is using before answering.
