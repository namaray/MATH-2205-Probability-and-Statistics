Correlation and Regression

1. What is Correlation?

Definition: Correlation is a statistical measure that describes the strength and direction of a relationship between two or more variables. It quantifies the direction and strength of the relationship between these variables.
📈

Positive Correlation

When one variable increases as the other increases

📉

Negative Correlation

When one decreases as the other increases

🔀

No Correlation

No consistent relationship between the two variables

2. Scatter Diagram / Scatterplot

Definition: Scatter plots (or scatter diagrams) are graphs that present the relationship between two variables in a dataset. They represent data points on a two-dimensional plane, i.e. on a Cartesian system.
  • The independent variable (or attribute) is plotted on the X-axis.
  • The dependent variable is plotted on the Y-axis.
Reading a Scatterplot

Points trending upward from left to right → positive correlation. Points trending downward → negative correlation. A cloud with no pattern → no correlation. The tighter the points cluster around a line, the stronger the correlation.

3. Correlation Coefficient (r)

Definition: Correlation is a statistical measure that describes the extent to which two variables are linearly related. The degree of association is measured by a correlation coefficient, denoted by \(r\). It is sometimes called Pearson's correlation coefficient.

The correlation coefficient is measured on a scale that varies from \(+1\) through \(0\) to \(-1\):

Range of \(r\)
\[-1 \leq r \leq +1\]
Pearson's Correlation Coefficient
\[r_{xy} = \frac{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})}{\sqrt{\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2 \; \sum (y_i - \bar{y})^2}}\]
Computational Form (easier by hand)
\[r = \frac{n\sum xy - (\sum x)(\sum y)}{\sqrt{\left[n\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2\right]\left[n\sum y^2 - (\sum y)^2\right]}}\]

Interpreting \(r\)

  • A linear correlation coefficient that is greater than zero indicates a positive relationship.
  • A value that is less than zero signifies a negative relationship.
  • A value of zero indicates no relationship between the two variables \(x\) and \(y\).
  • Complete (perfect) correlation between two variables is expressed by either \(+1\) or \(-1\).
Value of \(r\) Interpretation
\(r = +1\)Perfect positive linear relationship
\(0 < r < +1\)Positive relationship — stronger as \(r\) approaches \(+1\)
\(r = 0\)No linear relationship
\(-1 < r < 0\)Negative relationship — stronger as \(r\) approaches \(-1\)
\(r = -1\)Perfect negative linear relationship
⚠️ Remember

Correlation measures linear association only, and correlation does not imply causation.

4. Regression Analysis

Definition: Regression analysis is a technique of studying the dependence of one variable (called the dependent variable) on one or more variables (called explanatory variables) with a view to estimating or predicting the average value of the dependent variable in terms of the known or fixed values of the independent variable.

Types of Regression Analysis

📏

Linear Regression

One independent variable, straight-line relationship

🧮

Multiple Linear Regression

Two or more independent variables

〰️

Non-linear Regression

The relationship follows a curve, not a straight line

Correlation vs. Regression

Correlation tells you how strongly two variables move together — it is symmetric in \(x\) and \(y\). Regression gives you an equation to predict \(y\) from \(x\) — it is not symmetric.

5. Line of Best Fit

Definition: The line of best fit refers to a line through a scatter plot of data points that best expresses the relationship between those points.

6. Simple Regression Line — The Least Squares Method

There are many lines that could possibly be drawn on a given scatter diagram. Which one should we choose as the estimating line? Of course, the best fitted line. The most commonly used method for selecting such a line is the least-squares method, and the resulting line is called the least-squares line.

General Form of the Regression Line
\[y = a + bx\]

where \(a\) and \(b\) are constants to be found:

  • \(a\) = the intercept — the value of \(y\) when \(x = 0\)
  • \(b\) = the slope — the change in \(y\) for a one-unit increase in \(x\)
Slope \(b\)
\[b = \frac{n\sum xy - (\sum x)(\sum y)}{n\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2}\]
Intercept \(a\) — the line passes through \((\bar{x}, \bar{y})\)
\[\bar{y} = a + b\bar{x} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}\]

Procedure

Build the table

Compute the columns \(x\), \(y\), \(xy\) and \(x^2\), then their totals \(\sum x\), \(\sum y\), \(\sum xy\), \(\sum x^2\).

Find the slope

Substitute the totals into the formula for \(b\).

Find the intercept

Compute \(\bar{x}\) and \(\bar{y}\), then use \(a = \bar{y} - b\bar{x}\).

Write the line and predict

State \(y = a + bx\) and substitute any required value of \(x\).

"Least squares" means the line is chosen to minimise \(\sum (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2\) — the sum of squared vertical distances between the observed points and the line.

7. Extrapolation and Interpolation

Extrapolation is an estimation of a value based on extending a known sequence of values or facts beyond the area that is certainly known.
Interpolation is an estimation of a value within two known values in a sequence of values.
⚠️ Caution

Interpolation is generally reliable because it stays inside the observed range of the data. Extrapolation is risky — the relationship you fitted may not hold outside the range you actually measured.

8. Rank Correlation — Spearman's Coefficient

Definition: A rank correlation coefficient measures the degree of similarity between two rankings and can be used to assess the significance of the relation between them. Spearman's correlation coefficient (\(\rho\)) measures the strength and direction of association between two ranked variables.
Spearman's Rank Correlation Coefficient
\[\rho = 1 - \frac{6\sum d_i^2}{n(n^2 - 1)}\]

where:

  • \(\rho\) = Spearman's rank correlation coefficient
  • \(d_i\) = difference between the two ranks of each observation
  • \(n\) = number of observations
Range
\[-1 \leq \rho \leq +1\]

Interpretation & Comments

Spearman \(\rho\) Type of Correlation Comment
+1.00Perfect PositiveA perfect increasing monotonic relationship; ranks move in exactly the same direction.
+0.70 to +0.99Strong PositiveStrong tendency: as one variable increases in rank, the other does too.
+0.40 to +0.69Moderate PositiveModerate tendency of variables to increase together in rank.
+0.10 to +0.39Weak PositiveSlight tendency for higher values in one variable to be associated with higher ranks.
0No CorrelationNo relationship between the ranks of the two variables.
−0.10 to −0.39Weak NegativeSlight tendency: as one rank increases, the other decreases slightly.
−0.40 to −0.69Moderate NegativeModerate inverse relationship between ranks.
−0.70 to −0.99Strong NegativeStrong tendency: as one rank increases, the other decreases significantly.
−1.00Perfect NegativeA perfect decreasing monotonic relationship; ranks move in exactly opposite directions.

Procedure

Rank both variables

Assign ranks separately to the \(x\) values and the \(y\) values.

Find the differences

For each observation compute \(d = \text{rank}(x) - \text{rank}(y)\).

Square and sum

Compute \(d^2\) for each row and total them to get \(\sum d^2\).

Apply the formula

Substitute \(\sum d^2\) and \(n\) into \(\rho = 1 - \frac{6\sum d^2}{n(n^2-1)}\).

Use Spearman's \(\rho\) when the data are already ranks, when the relationship is monotonic but not linear, or when outliers would distort Pearson's \(r\).
