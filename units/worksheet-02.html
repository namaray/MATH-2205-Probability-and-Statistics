Part A — Continuous Random Variables

Question A1 — Mean and Variance

📝 Question

The time, in minutes, taken by students to answer a question in a multiple-choice test has a pdf \(f(t)\) as follows:

\[f(t) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{99}(40 - t^2), & 1 \leq t \leq 4 \\[6pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

Work out the mean and variance of the time taken.

Show solution
Mean
\[E(T) = \frac{1}{99}\int_1^4 t(40 - t^2)\,dt = \frac{1}{99}\left[20t^2 - \frac{t^4}{4}\right]_1^4\] \[= \frac{1}{99}\left[(320 - 64) - \left(20 - \tfrac{1}{4}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{99}\times 236.25 = \frac{105}{44} \approx 2.386\]
E(T²)
\[E(T^2) = \frac{1}{99}\int_1^4 (40t^2 - t^4)\,dt = \frac{1}{99}\left[\frac{40t^3}{3} - \frac{t^5}{5}\right]_1^4\] \[= \frac{1}{99}\left[\left(\frac{2560}{3} - \frac{1024}{5}\right) - \left(\frac{40}{3} - \frac{1}{5}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{99}\left(840 - 204.6\right) = \frac{353}{55} \approx 6.418\]
Variance
\[\text{Var}(T) = E(T^2) - [E(T)]^2 = \frac{353}{55} - \left(\frac{105}{44}\right)^2 = \frac{7003}{9680} \approx 0.723\]

Keep the exact fractions until the last line — rounding \(E(T)\) to 2.39 before squaring gives 0.706, which is noticeably off.

Mean ≈ 2.39 minutes · Variance ≈ 0.723 min²

Question A2 — Probability, Median and IQR

📝 Question

A continuous random variable, \(X\), has the following probability density function:

\[f(x) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{2}{25}(5 - x), & 0 \leq x \leq 5 \\[6pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]
  • (a) Find \(P(X \leq 2)\)
  • (b) Show that the median is \(5 - \dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{2}\)
  • (c) Calculate the interquartile range
Show solution
Get the cdf first — every part uses it
\[F(x) = \int_0^x \frac{2}{25}(5 - t)\,dt = \frac{2}{25}\left[5t - \frac{t^2}{2}\right]_0^x = \frac{10x - x^2}{25}\]
(a) P(X ≤ 2)
\[P(X \leq 2) = F(2) = \frac{20 - 4}{25} = \frac{16}{25} = 0.64\]
(b) Median — solve F(m) = 0.5
\[\frac{10m - m^2}{25} = \frac{1}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; m^2 - 10m + 12.5 = 0\] \[m = \frac{10 \pm \sqrt{100 - 50}}{2} = 5 \pm \frac{\sqrt{50}}{2}\]

The \(+\) root gives \(m \approx 8.54\), which is outside \(0 \leq x \leq 5\). So

\[m = 5 - \frac{\sqrt{50}}{2} \approx 1.464 \quad\text{as required.}\]
⚠️ Typo in the Worksheet

The sheet prints the answer as \(5 - \sqrt{\tfrac{(50)}{2}}\). Read literally that is \(5 - \sqrt{25} = 0\), which is wrong. It means \(5 - \dfrac{\sqrt{50}}{2}\), i.e. the square root of 50, then divided by 2 — equivalently \(5 - \sqrt{12.5}\).

(c) Interquartile range

Lower quartile — solve \(F(Q_1) = 0.25\):

\[Q_1^2 - 10Q_1 + 6.25 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; Q_1 = 5 - \frac{\sqrt{75}}{2} \approx 0.6699\]

Upper quartile — solve \(F(Q_3) = 0.75\):

\[Q_3^2 - 10Q_3 + 18.75 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; Q_3 = 5 - \frac{\sqrt{25}}{2} = 2.5\] \[IQR = Q_3 - Q_1 = 2.5 - 0.6699 = 1.830 \qquad\left(\text{exactly } \tfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} - 1)\right)\] (a) 0.64 · (b) m = 5 − √50⁄2 ≈ 1.464 · (c) IQR ≈ 1.83

Question A3 — Constant, Probability, Median and cdf

📝 Question

Two sisters make contact using an internet messaging service. The length of time for which they are logged on, in minutes, is modelled by the random variable \(T\) with probability density function:

\[f(t) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{k}(40 - t), & 10 \leq t \leq 30 \\[6pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

where \(k\) is a constant.

  • (a) Verify that \(k = 400\)
  • (b) Find the probability that the time they are logged on for is less than 15 minutes
  • (c) Calculate the median value for time they are logged on for
  • (d) Find the corresponding cdf of \(f(t)\)
Show solution
(a) Verify k = 400
\[\int_{10}^{30}(40 - t)\,dt = \left[40t - \frac{t^2}{2}\right]_{10}^{30} = (1200 - 450) - (400 - 50) = 400\]

So \(\frac{1}{k}\times 400 = 1\), giving \(k = 400\). ✓

(d first) The cdf — it makes (b) and (c) quick
\[F(t) = \frac{1}{400}\left[40u - \frac{u^2}{2}\right]_{10}^{t} = \frac{1}{400}\left(40t - \frac{t^2}{2} - 350\right) = \frac{80t - t^2 - 700}{800}\] \[F(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & t < 10 \\[4pt] \dfrac{80t - t^2 - 700}{800}, & 10 \leq t \leq 30 \\[4pt] 1, & t > 30 \end{cases}\]

Check: \(F(10) = \frac{800 - 100 - 700}{800} = 0\) ✓   \(F(30) = \frac{2400 - 900 - 700}{800} = 1\) ✓

(b) P(T < 15)
\[P(T < 15) = F(15) = \frac{1200 - 225 - 700}{800} = \frac{275}{800} = \frac{11}{32} \approx 0.344\]
(c) Median — solve F(m) = 0.5
\[\frac{80m - m^2 - 700}{800} = \frac{1}{2} \;\Longrightarrow\; m^2 - 80m + 1100 = 0\] \[m = \frac{80 \pm \sqrt{6400 - 4400}}{2} = 40 \pm \sqrt{500}\]

\(40 + \sqrt{500} \approx 62.4\) is outside \(10 \leq t \leq 30\), so

\[m = 40 - \sqrt{500} \approx 17.6 \text{ minutes}\] (a) k = 400 ✓ · (b) 11/32 ≈ 0.344 · (c) ≈ 17.6 min · (d) F(t) = (80t − t² − 700)/800 on [10, 30]

Question A4 — Constant, Probability, Mean and a Percentile

📝 Question

A random variable, \(X\), has probability density function given by:

\[f(x) = \begin{cases} k(1 - x), & -1 \leq x \leq 1 \\[4pt] 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}\]

where \(k\) is a constant.

  • (i) Show that \(k = \tfrac{1}{2}\)
  • (ii) Find \(P\!\left(X > \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\)
  • (iii) Find the mean of \(X\)
  • (iv) Find \(a\) such that \(P(X < a) = \tfrac{1}{4}\)
Show solution
(i) Show k = ½
\[\int_{-1}^{1} k(1 - x)\,dx = k\left[x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{1} = k\left[\left(1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right) - \left(-1 - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right] = 2k\]

\(2k = 1\), so \(k = \tfrac{1}{2}\). ✓

(ii) P(X > ½)
\[P\!\left(X > \tfrac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left[x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{1/2}^{1} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{3}{8}\right] = \frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{16}\]
(iii) Mean
\[E(X) = \frac{1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(x - x^2)\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_{-1}^{1} = \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\tfrac{1}{2} - \tfrac{1}{3}\right) - \left(\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3}\right)\right] = -\frac{1}{3}\]

A negative mean makes sense: \(1 - x\) is largest at \(x = -1\), so most of the probability sits on the left.

(iv) Find a with P(X < a) = ¼
\[\frac{1}{2}\left[x - \frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^{a} = \frac{1}{2}\left(a - \frac{a^2}{2} + \frac{3}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}\] \[a - \frac{a^2}{2} + 1 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a^2 - 2a - 2 = 0 \;\Longrightarrow\; a = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}\]

\(1 + \sqrt{3} \approx 2.73\) is outside \(-1 \leq x \leq 1\), so \(a = 1 - \sqrt{3}\).

(i) k = ½ ✓ · (ii) 1/16 · (iii) −1/3 · (iv) a = 1 − √3 ≈ −0.732

Part B — Estimation

📎 Method Recap (not yet in your slides)

A confidence interval is a range that captures the true population value with a stated confidence. Every interval here has the same shape: estimate ± z × standard error.

SituationConfidence interval
Mean, \(\sigma\) known\(\bar{x} \pm z\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}\)
Mean, \(\sigma\) unknown, large \(n\)\(\bar{x} \pm z\dfrac{s}{\sqrt{n}}\)
Proportion\(\hat{p} \pm z\sqrt{\dfrac{\hat{p}(1-\hat{p})}{n}}\)
Unbiased Estimates from Σx and Σx²
\[\bar{x} = \frac{\sum x}{n} \qquad s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\left(\sum x^2 - \frac{(\sum x)^2}{n}\right)\]
Confidence level90%95%98%99%
z1.6451.9602.3262.576
💡 Working Backwards from a Width

Width \(= 2 \times z \times SE\), so \(z = \dfrac{\text{width}}{2 \times SE}\). Then the confidence level is \(2\Phi(z) - 1\).

Question B1 — Sweets (σ known)

📝 Question

The label on a certain packet of sweets states the contents are 100 g. It is known that the standard deviation is 5 g. The mechanism producing these packets of sweets is checked. From a random sample of ten packets, the mean is 103.8 g. Find a 99% confidence interval for the mean contents of the packets of sweets. Use your result to explain whether the mechanism needs adjustment.

Show solution
Set up

\(\sigma = 5\) is known, \(n = 10\), \(\bar{x} = 103.8\), and \(z = 2.576\) for 99%.

Standard error and margin
\[SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{10}} = 1.5811 \qquad \text{margin} = 2.576 \times 1.5811 = 4.073\]
Interval
\[103.8 \pm 4.073 \;\Longrightarrow\; (99.73,\; 107.87)\]
Does the mechanism need adjustment?

The stated contents, 100 g, lies inside the interval. So at 99% confidence, a true mean of 100 g is entirely consistent with this sample — there is no evidence the mechanism needs adjustment.

Since \(\sigma\) is known we can use \(z\) even though \(n = 10\) is small, as long as the masses are normally distributed.

99% CI = (99.7 g, 107.9 g) · 100 g is inside → no adjustment needed

Question B2 — Quail Eggs (σ unknown)

📝 Question

The following data summarise the total mass, \(x\) grams, for a random sample of quail eggs:

\[n = 30, \quad \sum x = 254.4 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum x^2 = 2271.6\]
  • (a) Calculate a 99% confidence interval for the population mean
  • (b) An \(\alpha\)% confidence interval for the population mean, based on this sample, is found to have width of 1.3 grams. Find \(\alpha\).
Show solution
Unbiased estimates
\[\bar{x} = \frac{254.4}{30} = 8.48\] \[s^2 = \frac{1}{29}\left(2271.6 - \frac{254.4^2}{30}\right) = \frac{1}{29}(2271.6 - 2157.312) = \frac{114.288}{29} = 3.9410\] \[s = 1.9852 \qquad SE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1.9852}{\sqrt{30}} = 0.36244\]
(a) 99% interval
\[8.48 \pm 2.576 \times 0.36244 = 8.48 \pm 0.934 \;\Longrightarrow\; (7.55,\; 9.41)\]

\(\sigma\) is unknown, but \(n = 30\) is large, so by the Central Limit Theorem we can still use \(z\) with \(s\) in place of \(\sigma\).

(b) Find α from the width
\[z = \frac{\text{width}}{2 \times SE} = \frac{1.3}{2 \times 0.36244} = 1.793\] \[\Phi(1.793) = 0.9635 \;\Longrightarrow\; \text{confidence} = 2(0.9635) - 1 = 0.927\] (a) (7.55 g, 9.41 g) · (b) α ≈ 92.7

Question B3 — Pet Food

📝 Question

The following data summarise the masses, \(x\) kg, of 60 bags of dry pet food.

\[\sum x = 117 \quad\text{and}\quad \sum x^2 = 232.72\]
  • (a) Calculate unbiased estimates for the population mean and variance
  • (b) Calculate a 98% confidence interval for the population mean
  • (c) An \(\alpha\)% confidence interval for the population mean, based on this sample, is found to have width of 0.118 kg. Find \(\alpha\).
Show solution
(a) Unbiased estimates
\[\bar{x} = \frac{117}{60} = 1.95\] \[s^2 = \frac{1}{59}\left(232.72 - \frac{117^2}{60}\right) = \frac{1}{59}(232.72 - 228.15) = \frac{4.57}{59} = 0.07746\]

Divide by \(n - 1 = 59\), not \(n = 60\) — that's what makes it the unbiased estimate.

(b) 98% interval
\[SE = \sqrt{\frac{0.07746}{60}} = 0.035930\] \[1.95 \pm 2.326 \times 0.035930 = 1.95 \pm 0.0836 \;\Longrightarrow\; (1.866,\; 2.034)\]
(c) Find α from the width
\[z = \frac{0.118}{2 \times 0.035930} = 1.642 \qquad \Phi(1.642) = 0.9497\] \[\text{confidence} = 2(0.9497) - 1 = 0.899\] (a) mean = 1.95 kg, variance ≈ 0.0775 kg² · (b) (1.87 kg, 2.03 kg) · (c) α ≈ 90

Question B4 — Jeans (a proportion)

📝 Question

A quality control check of a random sample of 120 pairs of jeans produced at a factory finds that 24 pairs are sub-standard. Calculate the following confidence intervals for the proportion of jeans produced that are sub-standard:

  • (a) a 90% confidence interval
  • (b) a 98% confidence interval
Show solution
Sample proportion and standard error
\[\hat{p} = \frac{24}{120} = 0.2 \qquad SE = \sqrt{\frac{0.2 \times 0.8}{120}} = 0.036515\]
(a) 90% interval
\[0.2 \pm 1.645 \times 0.036515 = 0.2 \pm 0.0601 \;\Longrightarrow\; (0.140,\; 0.260)\]
(b) 98% interval
\[0.2 \pm 2.326 \times 0.036515 = 0.2 \pm 0.0849 \;\Longrightarrow\; (0.115,\; 0.285)\]

The 98% interval is wider: to be more confident of capturing the true proportion, you need a bigger net.

(a) (0.140, 0.260) · (b) (0.115, 0.285)

Part C — Hypothesis Testing

📎 Method Recap (not yet in your slides)

A hypothesis test asks: if nothing had changed, how surprising is what we observed? If it's surprising enough — less likely than the significance level — we reject "nothing changed".

Define the random variable and its distribution

e.g. \(X \sim B(20, p)\) or \(X \sim \text{Po}(\lambda)\)

State the hypotheses

\(H_0\): the old value (\(p = 0.4\)). \(H_1\): the claimed change — \(p < 0.4\) for "lower", \(p > 0.4\) for "higher" (one-tailed), or \(p \neq 0.4\) for "different" (two-tailed).

Find the probability of a result at least this extreme

Assuming \(H_0\) is true. For "lower", find \(P(X \leq \text{observed})\).

Compare with the significance level

Probability \(<\) level → reject \(H_0\). Probability \(\geq\) level → do not reject \(H_0\).

Conclude in context

Say what it means for the actual situation — not just "reject \(H_0\)".

⚠️ Wording Matters

Never say "\(H_0\) is true" or "we accept \(H_0\)". The correct phrasing is "there is insufficient evidence to reject \(H_0\)" — failing to find evidence isn't proof of no effect.

Question C1 — Racetrack Simulator

📝 Question

Experience has shown that drivers on a Formula 1 racetrack simulator crash 40% of the time. Zander decides to run a simulator training programme to reduce the number of crashes. To evaluate the effectiveness of the training programme, Zander allows 20 drivers, one by one, to use the simulator. Zander counts how many of the drivers crash. Four drivers crash. Test the effectiveness of Zander's simulation training programme at the 5% level of significance.

Show solution
Random variable and hypotheses

Let \(X\) = number of the 20 drivers who crash, so \(X \sim B(20, p)\).

\[H_0: p = 0.4 \qquad H_1: p < 0.4 \quad\text{(one-tailed: the programme is meant to reduce crashes)}\]
Probability of 4 or fewer crashes, assuming H₀
\[P(X \leq 4) = \sum_{r=0}^{4}\binom{20}{r}(0.4)^r(0.6)^{20-r}\]
r01234
P(X = r)0.000040.000490.003090.012350.03499
\[P(X \leq 4) = 0.0510\]
Compare and conclude

\(0.0510 > 0.05\), so we do not reject \(H_0\).

There is insufficient evidence at the 5% level that the training programme reduces the crash rate.

A Close Call

This misses significance by about 0.001. Had only 3 drivers crashed, \(P(X \leq 3) = 0.0160\) and the result would have been significant. A larger sample would give the test a better chance of detecting a real improvement.

Question C2 — The Offside Rule

📝 Question

It is claimed that 40% of professional footballers cannot explain the offside rule. Alberto thinks the percentage is lower. To test this, he asks 15 professional footballers to explain the offside rule. Define the random variable and hypotheses. Carry out a hypothesis test at the 5% significance level given that two of the professional footballers asked cannot explain the rule.

Show solution
Random variable and hypotheses

Let \(X\) = number of the 15 footballers who cannot explain the offside rule, so \(X \sim B(15, p)\), where \(p\) is the proportion of all professional footballers who cannot explain it.

\[H_0: p = 0.4 \qquad H_1: p < 0.4\]
Probability of 2 or fewer, assuming H₀
\[P(X \leq 2) = (0.6)^{15} + 15(0.4)(0.6)^{14} + \binom{15}{2}(0.4)^2(0.6)^{13}\] \[= 0.00047 + 0.00470 + 0.02194 = 0.0271\]
Compare and conclude

\(0.0271 < 0.05\), so we reject \(H_0\).

There is significant evidence at the 5% level that fewer than 40% of professional footballers cannot explain the offside rule — Alberto's belief is supported.

P(X ≤ 2) = 0.0271 < 0.05 → reject H₀ · evidence the percentage is lower than 40%

Question C3 — Road Accidents (Poisson)

📝 Question

Accidents on a stretch of road occur at the rate of seven each month. New traffic measures are put in place to reduce the number of accidents. In the following month, there are only two accidents.

  • (a) Test at the 5% level of significance if there is evidence that the new traffic measures have significantly reduced the number of accidents.
  • (b) Over a period of 6 months, there are 32 accidents. It is claimed the new traffic measures are no longer reducing the number of accidents.
    • (i) Test this claim at the 5% level of significance.
    • (ii) What would your conclusion be if you tested the claim at the 10% level of significance?
Show solution
(a) One month — random variable and hypotheses

Let \(X\) = number of accidents in one month, \(X \sim \text{Po}(\lambda)\).

\[H_0: \lambda = 7 \qquad H_1: \lambda < 7\]
(a) Probability of 2 or fewer, assuming H₀
\[P(X \leq 2) = e^{-7}\left(1 + 7 + \frac{7^2}{2}\right) = 32.5\,e^{-7} = 0.0296\]

\(0.0296 < 0.05\), so reject \(H_0\). There is significant evidence at the 5% level that the new measures have reduced the number of accidents.

(b) Six months — scale the rate

If the measures are no longer working, the rate is back to 7 per month, i.e. \(7 \times 6 = 42\) over 6 months. Let \(Y\) = accidents in 6 months, \(Y \sim \text{Po}(\lambda)\).

\[H_0: \lambda = 42 \;\;\text{(measures no longer reducing accidents)} \qquad H_1: \lambda < 42 \;\;\text{(still reducing)}\]
(b) Probability of 32 or fewer — normal approximation

\(\lambda = 42\) is too large for Poisson tables, so approximate with \(N(42, 42)\) — Poisson has mean = variance. Use a continuity correction: \(Y \leq 32\) becomes \(Y < 32.5\).

\[z = \frac{32.5 - 42}{\sqrt{42}} = -1.466 \qquad P(Y \leq 32) \approx \Phi(-1.466) = 0.0713\]

(The exact Poisson value is 0.0668. It leads to the same conclusions below.)

(b)(i) At the 5% level

\(0.0713 > 0.05\), so do not reject \(H_0\). There is insufficient evidence at the 5% level that the measures are still reducing accidents — the claim cannot be rejected.

(b)(ii) At the 10% level

\(0.0713 < 0.10\), so reject \(H_0\). At the 10% level there is evidence that the measures are still reducing accidents, so the claim would be rejected.

Why the Answers Differ

The significance level is how much evidence you demand before rejecting \(H_0\). A probability of 0.07 isn't rare enough for a strict 5% test, but it is rare enough for a more lenient 10% test. Same data, different standard.

(a) 0.0296 < 0.05 → reject H₀, measures reduced accidents · (b)(i) 0.0713 > 0.05 → do not reject, claim stands · (b)(ii) 0.0713 < 0.10 → reject, claim rejected
